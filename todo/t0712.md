- **🐛 [HIGH — five live self-host miscompiles, one of them a deterministic SIGBUS on ordinary beginner code; Rust gg is correct on all five, so under Core #8 they are defects on the lagging lane. NEEDS A SCOUT BEFORE A BRIEF — the previous diagnosis was measured FALSE.] The self-host mis-types the OUTER LINK of a method chain over a user-defined receiver.** The parser gives every link of a postfix spine the same `span.start`, so `expr_types[span.start]` collapses the whole chain onto one entry; `lower_types.gg:lookup_expr_gir_type` reads `expr_link_types[span.end]` FIRST and falls back to that contended key, so a link whose ladder arm never published gets typed with its RECEIVER.

    It is also false BY CONSTRUCTION, which is the part that should have been caught in five minutes: **none of the five methods was on the gated name list.** `split` has its own arm (`infer.gg`), `len`/`count` has its own arm, `keys` and `get` were ALREADY receiver-gated (and already answer correctly), and `bytes` has **no arm at all** (`grep -n '"bytes"' infer.gg` → no hit). A gate on a list cannot change behaviour for a method that is not on the list.

  - **THE ACTUAL ROOT CAUSE, and the positive finding this item now carries.** The publishable set is "RESOLVED, never GUESSED", and the obstacle to widening it is a **TYPECHECKER-TYPE vs LOWERING-TYPE conflict**, not receiver typing. `infer.gg`'s `clone | copy | sorted | reversed | filter` arm answers with the RECEIVER's type — which is the *correct typechecker answer*, `Vector[int]` — while the lowerer needs the *lowering* type, `FilterIter`. Since `lookup_expr_gir_type` reads `expr_link_types` FIRST, publishing the typechecker's truth OVERRIDES a fallback that was reconstructing the lowering type correctly. That is why the single-exit publish regressed rows that have nothing to do with method admission.
