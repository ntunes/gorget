- **🆕⚖ [MED — A DECISION, NOT A BUG FIX; the half of the shipped-compiler orphan that R47 Track F4a deliberately did NOT take]**

R47 F4a closed two leaks in `gg test --parallel` (`src/main.rs`): the wait loop no longer abandons un-waited workers when it exits early, and neither does the spawn-failure path twelve lines above it. Both now call `proc_guard::kill_process_tree`. **What remains is that a killed worker's OWN grandchildren still orphan**, because the workers are not spawned into their own process group:

```
grep -n process_group src/main.rs   →   no matches
```

`kill_process_tree` signals the NEGATIVE pid to reach the whole tree, and that only works when the target LEADS the group. F4a added a `getpgid(pid) == pid` guard to the helper so the call degrades to a correct direct-child kill instead of aiming a signal at a group the child does not lead — but degrade it does. A worker that forks (a Gorget test spawning a subprocess, say) leaves that subprocess behind.

- **⚠ WHY THIS IS FILED RATHER THAN FIXED: `process_group(0)` AT THE SPAWN SITE IS USER-VISIBLE.** It detaches the workers from the terminal's foreground process group, so **Ctrl-C stops reaching them** and `gg` would have to catch SIGINT and forward it to the group itself. That is a decision about what `gg test --parallel` promises an interactive user, not a harness cleanup, and it deserves to be made deliberately. The two candidate shapes:
  1. **`process_group(0)` + a SIGINT handler in `gg`** that forwards to each worker group. Correct tree kill, correct Ctrl-C, more moving parts, and the handler must be re-entrant-safe.
  2. **Leave the workers in `gg`'s group.** Ctrl-C keeps working by inheritance; a killed worker's grandchildren keep leaking. This is today's behaviour.
- **⊕ AND WHILE THAT DECISION IS BEING MADE, TWO SMALLER ONES IN THE SAME LOOP:** `child.wait()` at `src/main.rs:3617` has **no deadline**, so `gg test --parallel` with one hung worker hangs forever (the `t0842` class, in the product); and the `Vec<Child>` has **no `Drop`**, so a panic anywhere between the first spawn and the last wait leaks every worker. A `Drop` impl that tree-kills is the cheap structural fix and is independent of the Ctrl-C question.

- **⚠ THE `src/backend/c/runtime/process_spawn_runtime.c` DECISION IS THE SAME SHAPE AND SHOULD BE TAKEN TOGETHER** — see `todo/t0842` (A). That file `fork()`s with no `setpgid`, kills the direct child only, and documents a timeout that explicitly does NOT kill. Both are "what does the product promise about a child process it gives up on", and answering one without the other will produce two different answers.
